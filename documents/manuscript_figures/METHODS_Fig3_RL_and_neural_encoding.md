### Fig3E_RL_latents

**Shows.** Four heat maps of the fitted agent's internal variables over one session, each plotted as trials (rows, top = trial 1, 120 trials) against the 50 spatial bins (columns): value `V(b)`, the actor reward-prediction error, perceptual precision `1/sigma`, and the lick rate `lambda`. A dashed blue vertical line marks the visual landmark (bin 20; 80 a.u. = 100 cm) and two solid green lines the reward-zone boundaries (bins 25 and 33.75; 100–135 a.u. = 125–169 cm). The point of the panel is that the three regressors handed to the neural analysis have distinct spatiotemporal signatures: value is a reward-zone bump that *sharpens progressively across the whole session* while the broad corridor-wide baseline decays; the actor RPE is a predominantly negative field, largest and most variable inside the reward zone, whose RZ negativity *intensifies* across trials as early positive excursions at bins 25–27 disappear; precision is a landmark-locked ridge that is essentially frozen across trials. **This panel is a synthetic mouse, not a recorded one** — it is drawn from the ground-truth parameter-recovery cohort (12 mice x 120 trials), not from any of the 16 task mice, and the figure title's "mouse 1" refers to synthetic mouse 1.

**Methods.** The agent was a belief-state, two-timescale actor–critic implemented in JAX (`Striatum project/rl_model/rl_model/agent.py`). The environment mirrored `project_cfg.m`: a 200 a.u. corridor discretised into 50 bins of 4 a.u. (5 cm), landmark at 80 a.u., reward zone 100–135 a.u., so that latents aligned 1:1 with the neural `spatial_binned_fr` tensor (`config.py:15-27`). *Perception* was a Kalman filter over corridor position: observation noise grew linearly with distance from the landmark, `R(x) = R_slope*|x - 80| + R0`, giving Kalman gain `K = sigma_pred/(sigma_pred + R)` and posterior `sigma_post = (1-K)*sigma_pred` (`agent.py:233-236`); the belief handed to the value function was a normalised Gaussian over the 50 bins, `b = softmax(-0.5*(centres-mu)^2/max(sigma, 1e-3))` (`agent.py:155-158`). Between bins the belief was propagated with speed-dependent path-integration noise `Q_eff = Q*(1 + kappa_v*v)` (`agent.py:280-281`), which is what makes running speed consequential for perceptual accuracy; between trials the terminal posterior variance was carried over and **additively incremented** by `iti_inflation` (`agent.py:327`), the first trial starting from a fixed `sigma0 = 400 a.u.^2` (SD 20 a.u. = 25 cm; `config.py:52`, `agent.py:337-339`). The `precision` latent was `1/max(sigma_post, 1e-3)` (`agent.py:314`). *Appraisal* was a linear critic on the belief, `V(b) = w_crit . b`, initialised flat at a fitted `w_init` (the residual value carried in from the animals' prior random-reward task) and updated by reward-only TD, `w_crit += eta_w * delta_critic * b` with `delta_critic = r + gamma*V' - V` (`agent.py:288, 293, 337`). *Control* was two actors, both initialised at zero and both driven by a second, cost-bearing TD error `delta_actor = (r - c_lick*lick - rho*dt) + gamma*V' - V` (`agent.py:289-290`); because the critic never modelled the costs, `delta_actor` retained a persistent negative component wherever the animal acted unprofitably, and this quantity — not `delta_critic` — was exported as the `rpe` latent (`agent.py:313`). The lick actor used a three-factor rule `w_lick += eta_a * delta_actor * lick * b` (`agent.py:298`); the velocity actor used a deterministic gradient `w_vel += eta_a * g_vel * b` (`agent.py:308`), with `g_vel = d(expected per-bin advantage)/d(log v)` in closed form (`agent.py:188-206`), pinned to `jax.grad` of `_vel_advantage` by `tests/test_recovery.py:88 test_velocity_grad_matches_autodiff`. *Policies*: the lick policy set a **rate** `lambda = lambda_max * sigmoid(beta*V + w_lick.b - theta) + 1e-9` licks/s and the per-bin count was `Poisson(lambda * dt)`; the velocity policy was log-normal, `log v ~ Normal(v_base + v_slope*V + w_vel.b, sigma_v)` (`agent.py:245-266`). Critically, `dt = 5 cm / v` (`agent.py:257`), and because the observed velocity was itself derived as `5 cm / duration` in `io_real.py:60`, `dt` reduced exactly to the animal's observed dwell time in that bin except where the 2–150 cm/s velocity clip bound (`io_real.py:22, 61`): the lick likelihood was therefore a Poisson **exposure** model, `Poisson(count | rate x observed dwell time)`, so an animal that ran fast through a bin had proportionally less opportunity to lick. Reward was graded and saturating, `r_total = R*(1 - exp(-cumulative RZ licks / K_REWARD))` with `K_REWARD = 10` and `R = reward_magnitude = 1` fixed, the per-bin reward being that total's increment (`agent.py:273-275`; `config.py:39, 53`), so speed traded off against water collected. *Data*: behaviour was read from `processed_data/preprocessed_data5cm.mat` per mouse as per-bin lick counts and dwell durations; each session was truncated at the behaviourally defined disengagement point (`change_point_mean`), velocity was clipped to 2–150 cm/s, and bins with non-finite licks or non-positive duration were masked out (`io_real.py:51-62`). Retained session lengths were **27–225 trials (median 100)** across the 16 task mice. *Fitting*: 16 free subjective parameters per mouse (`config.py:74-100`), with `c_lick = 0.025` and `K_REWARD = 10` held fixed because `c_lick` degenerates with `eta_a` (only their product is identifiable; recovery v4 — `config.py:29-39`). Fitting was **teacher-forced maximum a posteriori, not maximum likelihood** — the negative log-likelihood plus a broad isotropic Gaussian prior (SD 4.0 in the unconstrained space, centred on the default `typical` parameters) purely to stop unidentified directions drifting to the box bounds (`fitting.py:39-45, 49`) — minimised by L-BFGS-B on exact JAX gradients within a ±12 box (`fitting.py:17`), with **4 restarts** for the real fits (`fit_real_data.py:72`): the first from the default parameters and the remaining three perturbed by SD 0.5 in the unconstrained space (`fitting.py:96`). `maxiter` was 250 for sessions >200 trials and 400 otherwise (`fit_real_data.py:153`). Cross-validation was **interleaved by default** (every 5th trial dropped from the likelihood, `TEST_EVERY = 5`); a forward/blocked scheme holding out the trailing 25% of trials is implemented as `RLMODEL_CV=forward` and written to a separate directory (`fit_real_data.py:56-88`). Held-out trials were excluded from the objective (`score_mask = 0`) but retained in the learning updates (`learn_mask = 1`) so the agent's within-session trajectory at a held-out trial was the trajectory the animal actually had (`fit_real_data.py:143-149`). Against a saturated per-bin null (per-bin Poisson rate plus per-bin log-normal velocity estimated on the training trials, `fit_real_data.py:103-124`), the interleaved fits gained a mean **+0.604 nats/bin** on the lick channel with 16/16 mice positive but **−0.064 nats/bin on the velocity channel with only 3/16 positive**; the forward-CV fits gained +0.454 (15/16) and +0.057 (7/16) respectively — but the forward velocity *median* is **−0.016**, i.e. the positive mean is outlier-driven (recomputed from `results/real_fits_v6_{interleaved,forward}/M*.npz`). Real-mouse latents were exported per (trial x bin) to `results/rl_latents.{mat,npz}` from `real_fits_v6_interleaved` (`plot_real_data.py:36, 129-146`). The panel itself was rendered by `run_parameter_recovery.py:247-273`, called on `true_lat0` at line 147 — i.e. the true-parameter latents of synthetic mouse 1 of a 12-mouse, 120-trial cohort (`run_parameter_recovery.py:58-59, 83`).

### Fig3E_RL_latent_recovery

**Shows.** Five scatter plots, one per exported latent (value, RPE, precision, lick rate, expected velocity), of the latent computed with the **true** generative parameters (x) against the latent computed with the **fitted** parameters (y) on the same simulated behaviour, one point per (trial x bin) subsampled to 4000 points, with the identity line dashed. Titles carry the cohort-mean Pearson r: value 0.996, RPE 0.994, precision **0.915**, lick rate 0.999, expected velocity 0.999. This is the TDD gate that licenses using the fitted latents as neural regressors: it establishes that even where individual subjective parameters are poorly identified, the quantities actually regressed against neurons are recovered. Value, lick rate and velocity form tight near-linear bands (value sitting marginally below identity, velocity marginally above); precision does not — its cloud is broken into four or five discontinuous streaks displaced well above identity, so the fitted precision is a monotone but biased and piecewise-distorted version of the truth.

**Methods.** A synthetic cohort of 12 mice x 120 trials was generated by rolling the agent out generatively (the agent samples its own licks and velocities, `agent.py:379-387`) from parameter vectors drawn by adding Gaussian jitter of SD 0.35 in the unconstrained space to the `typical` values, with a fixed cohort seed of 2026 so the cohort regenerates identically on every invocation (`synthetic.py:17-26, 40-43`; `run_parameter_recovery.py:58-60, 83`). Each synthetic mouse was then fitted by the same MAP/L-BFGS-B objective as the real mice (prior SD 4.0, ±12 box, `maxiter = 400`) but with a **single restart** from the defaults (restart 1 reliably wins on synthetic data; the real fits use 4) and, unlike the real fits, with **no validity mask and no held-out trials** — the synthetic likelihood is the full session (`run_parameter_recovery.py:60, 95-96`). Recovery was assessed in two ways. Parameter recovery was the across-cohort Pearson correlation of true against fitted values **in natural space**, computed by mapping both `u_true` and `u_fit` through `to_constrained` and correlating (`run_parameter_recovery.py:73-78, 115-119`); from `results/recovery_v6.npz` the value/policy parameters recovered well — `log_sigma_v` 1.000, `v_base` 0.998, `eta_w` 0.989, `gamma` 0.974, `lambda_max` 0.969, `theta` 0.953, `v_slope` 0.945, `beta` 0.933, `w_init` 0.887, `eta_a` 0.886, `rho` 0.761 — whereas **every perceptual parameter failed the script's own r ≥ 0.6 threshold except `R0`**: `R0` 0.717, `Q` 0.524, `R_slope` 0.294, `kappa_v` −0.153, `iti_inflation` −0.063. Latent recovery, the quantity this panel reports, was computed per mouse as the Pearson correlation between `session_latents(u_true, ...)` and `session_latents(u_fit, ...)` evaluated on the *same* behavioural input, then averaged over the 12 mice (`run_parameter_recovery.py:121-134`); the scatter shows synthetic mouse 1 while the title r is the cohort mean. Verified from `results/recovery_v6.npz`: value 0.9964 (worst mouse 0.9895), RPE 0.9941 (0.9823), lick rate 0.9992 (0.9980), expected velocity 0.9988 (0.9977), **precision 0.9153 (worst mouse 0.5858)**. The figure was written by `run_parameter_recovery.py:194-216`, which colours a title green at r ≥ 0.9 — precision clears that threshold and is rendered as passing.

### Fig3F_neural_encoding_of_latents

**Shows.** A 2x3 grid, one axis per recorded area (ACC n = 699 cells, DLS 522, DMS 394, V1 264, CA1 133, DG 18), each showing the percentage of neurons with a significant unique cross-validated contribution (dR2) for each of the three latents — value, RPE, precision — under two models: behaviour-controlled (blue) and additionally spatial-controlled (red, hatched), with a dashed line at the 5% chance rate. **Value and RPE are encoded above chance in DMS, DLS and ACC; precision is essentially at chance, the one exception being a marginal DMS cell (median 8.6%, p_BH = 0.032) that is more likely an artefact than a result** (see caveats). In ACC, DMS, DLS and V1 the value and RPE bars *rise* when stationary spatial tuning is removed (e.g. DLS RPE 17.0% → 33.3% cell-pooled), i.e. those latent contributions are not a repackaging of place tuning; the pattern reverses for precision in DLS (8.2% → 6.1%) and V1 (9.1% → 8.3%) and for RPE in CA1 (30.1% → 29.3%). V1 shows the numerically largest fractions of any area but has only 5 animals, so its test bottoms out at the signed-rank floor (p = 0.0625); CA1 and DG (n = 3 each) were not tested at all, and DG (18 cells) is uninterpretable. The honest headline is narrow: a *fraction of cells* passes a shuffle test, while per-cell effect sizes are ~1e-4 of variance, so this is evidence that value and RPE are represented at all, not evidence that they are represented strongly.

**Methods.** *Input.* Per-neuron firing was the z-scored spatially binned rate tensor `z_spatial_binned_fr_all`, z-scored per neuron jointly over bins and trials for the whole session (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:193`, `nan_zscore(spatial_binned_fr_all, [2,3])` on a cells x bins x trials array), read as (trials x bins x cells) and paired per mouse with the exported RL latents from `results/rl_latents.npz` (`neural_encoding.py:29-47`; `run_neural_encoding.py:99-128`). Cells were labelled by the `is_acc/is_dms/is_dls/is_v1/is_ca1/is_dg` masks; all 2030 cells carried a label and the six area counts sum exactly to 2030. Each mouse's behavioural and neural records were clamped to `min(n_behavioural_trials, n_neural_trials)` before analysis (`run_neural_encoding.py:115-128`). *Design.* For each mouse, valid (trial, bin) samples were stacked into rows — 27 to 225 trials x 50 bins, i.e. roughly 1.3k–11k rows per mouse before masking. The regressors of interest were the three latents `value`, `rpe`, `precision`; the nuisance covariates were the **observed** lick count, the **observed** velocity (`exp(logv)`), and a smooth trial-drift basis of `n_drift = 5` DCT cosines `cos(pi*k*t)`, `k = 1..5`, over the trial index and constant across bins (`neural_encoding.py:120-123, 147-149`), so that a latent could never be credited for generic slow session drift. Note the model's own policy outputs (`lick_rate`, `v_mean`) were *not* regressors — the observed behaviour was used instead. *Two models.* `'beh'` globally demeaned every column; `'beh_spatial'` instead subtracted, per spatial bin, that bin's mean over trials from firing, latents and nuisances alike (`_demean_bin`, `neural_encoding.py:63-71, 152-159`), so a latent was credited only for trial-by-trial modulation beyond stationary spatial tuning. After the demeaning step, the **regressor** columns (latents and nuisances) were z-scored; the firing matrix was demeaned but not re-scaled, being already per-neuron z-scored upstream (`neural_encoding.py:155-158`). *Estimation.* Ridge regression with `lambda = 1.0` on z-scored columns against thousands of rows per mouse — shrinkage of order 1e-4 to 1e-3, effectively OLS — solved vectorised over all neurons at once, with 5-fold **trial-wise** cross-validation: fold labels were assigned as `arange(n_trials) % 5` and shuffled with a fixed seed (`seed = 0`), then broadcast to all bins of a trial, so no trial ever straddled the train/test split (`neural_encoding.py:102-114, 142-144`). *Readout.* Per latent, the unique cross-validated contribution `dR2 = R2(full) - R2(full minus that latent)`, the reduced model retaining the other two latents and all nuisances (`neural_encoding.py:163-167`). *Nulls.* Two permutation nulls with `n_shuffle = 50` each: a **trial shuffle** (permute the latent's trial order, preserving its spatial profile) and a **bin shuffle** (permute its bins, preserving its trial course); the null design matrix replaced only that latent's column and was re-prepped and re-z-scored identically, and p was `(#{shuffle dR2 >= observed} + 1)/51` (`neural_encoding.py:169-185`). *Gate.* Before touching real data, a synthetic ground-truth check required that neurons built from value, RPE and precision were detected and that a neuron driven purely by the lick nuisance was not (`run_neural_encoding.py:48-91`, run at `n_shuffle = 200`); all six checks passed on the 2026-08-12 run (log at `results/run_neural_encoding_2026-08-12b.log`). *Aggregation and inference.* Per (mouse x area x model x latent), the mean dR2 and the fraction of that animal's neurons with trial-shuffle p < 0.05 were recorded; - **Cells per animal are wildly unequal, so the animal-level Wilcoxon weights a 2-cell estimate as heavily as a 237-cell one.** DMS: 2–69 cells/animal; DLS: 2–237 (M05 alone contributes 237 of 522 DLS cells but is 1 of 11 in the test); ACC: 5–175; V1: 7–102; CA1: 24–81; DG: 1–11.
- **The DMS precision result should still not be reported as precision coding, but the session-length argument for that does not hold in DMS.** The structural problem is real: in 13 of 16 mice the precision latent retains <0.1% of its variance after per-bin demeaning, yet `_zscore` (`neural_encoding.py:53-56`) rescales that residue to unit variance before it enters the `beh_spatial` design — so the regressor tested there is an amplified remnant of the early-session Kalman transient, not the precision signal, and its dR2 is structurally uninterpretable regardless of biology (this is exactly what `spatial_demean_var_ratio` was written to flag, `neural_encoding.py:74-99`). **However**, the supporting correlation with session length holds only at the whole-mouse, all-area pooled level (Spearman rho = −0.554, p = 0.026, n = 16, versus +0.527, p = 0.036 for value — exploratory and uncorrected). Recomputed *within DMS*, the area whose cell is BH-significant, precision gives rho = −0.196, p = 0.466 and value +0.313, p = 0.238 — neither significant. The "short sessions manufacture DMS precision significance" claim is **not supported by the DMS data**; the strongest area-specific version is ACC (rho = −0.600, p = 0.018), whose precision cell is not significant anyway. Report the variance-retention argument, not the session-length one. `plot_encoding_detail.py:45` already routes precision to the `'beh'` model for the variance reason, but the CSV, the panel and the BH-significant DMS cell do not.
- **Effect sizes are negligible.** Unique dR2 of ~1e-4 against a full-model CV R2 of ~1% means the latents explain of order one part in a hundred of the explainable variance. The claim rests entirely on the *fraction of cells* clearing a permutation threshold. Note also that `_cv_r2` computes `sst` against the full-sample mean of Y rather than the training mean (`neural_encoding.py:112-114`), a small optimism in the reported `r2_full`; it cancels in the dR2 difference.
- **V1's p = 0.0625 is the Wilcoxon floor at n = 5**, not a null: with five animals the smallest attainable two-sided signed-rank p is 1/16. V1 has the highest median fractions of any area (value 29.2%) and simply cannot be resolved at this n. CA1 and DG (n = 3) fell below the script's n >= 5 gate and were not tested.
- **The permutation null is coarse.** With `n_shuffle = 50` the attainable p-values are multiples of 1/51, so "p < 0.05" means no shuffle of 50 exceeded the observed dR2, and the smallest attainable p is 1/51 = 2.0%. Each neuron's p is therefore estimated from 50 draws, which is thin.
- **The bin-shuffle null is computed and stored but never used.** `pvalbin_*` is written to every per-mouse file (`run_neural_encoding.py:135`) yet neither the figure nor `aggregate_encoding.py` reads it, so the panel's implicit claim that the effect is trial-by-trial rather than spatial rests only on the `beh_spatial` demeaning, not on that second null.
- **Area labels are assigned by overwriting.** `neural_encoding.py:43-45` writes each area mask in turn into one label array, so any cell appearing in two `is_*` masks would silently be assigned the last-written area (DG). Counts sum to 2030 with no `none` cells, but overlap is not tested for.
- **`beh` and `beh_spatial` do not agree for every cell shown.** DMS value is significant only under `beh_spatial` (p = 0.147 under `beh`), and no V1 or CA1/DG cell is significant under either. A caption that says "value is encoded in DMS" is a `beh_spatial`-only claim.
- **Mild circularity in the regressor construction.** The latents were produced by a model fitted to the whole session (held-out trials still drove teacher-forced learning by design), so the encoding model's cross-validation folds are not independent of how the regressors were built. The leakage is small but not zero, and it is the same class of defect as the session-global position-decoder template used elsewhere in the manuscript.
- **Firing rates carry the unfixed occupancy defect.** `spatial_binning.m:45` computes dwell time as the span between the first and last sample in a bin, i.e. `(k-1)` rather than `k` intervals, and `spatial_binning.m:66` divides spike counts by it, so rates are inflated in a speed-dependent way. Observed velocity is a nuisance covariate here, which partially absorbs this, but the same defective durations also generate the velocity regressor, so the control is not independent of the contaminant.
- **No learning claim is supported by this panel.** The encoding was fit over whole sessions with a drift basis regressed out; nothing here is resolved by Naive/Intermediate/Expert epoch, and no LP-shuffled null exists.
