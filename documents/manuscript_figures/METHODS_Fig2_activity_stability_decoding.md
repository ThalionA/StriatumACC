### Fig2B_recording_examples

**Shows.** No panel exists. The intended panel is example spike rasters and mean waveforms illustrating the cell classes used elsewhere in the figure. No live code produces it.

**Methods.** Had it been built, the classes would be those returned by `classify_neuron_types.m`, which reads four waveform/ISI features stored per mouse in `RawData/<mouse>_neurontype2025.mat` and `<mouse>_v1_neurontype2025.mat`: col 1 mean firing rate (Hz), col 2 proportion of long ISIs, col 3 peak-to-trough time (ms), col 4 a post-spike ISI metric (documented at `classify_neuron_types.m:6-11`; col 1 is not used by either rule, `:53-57`). The narrow/broad boundary is `pt_threshold_ms = 0.4` by default (`:36-38`). Striatal units (DMS, DLS) keep a four-way rule: broad and ISI metric <= 40 -> MSN (1); narrow and long-ISI proportion < 0.1 -> FSN (2); broad and ISI metric > 40 -> TAN (3); narrow and long-ISI proportion >= 0.1 -> UIN (4) (`:65-68`). ACC, V1, CA1, DG and any other area take a binary split on the same boundary: FS (2) if peak-to-trough < 0.4 ms, RS (5) otherwise (`:72-73`). Units with a non-finite peak-to-trough stay NaN (`:61`). Counts after the 2026-08-12 fix (`NOTES.md`): ACC 76 FS / 623 RS, V1 42 / 222, CA1 17 / 116, DG 7 / 11.

**Caveats.**
- No figure and no code; the classification is verifiable only from the classifier source and the counts recorded in `NOTES.md`.
- The classifier was broken in three independent ways until 2026-08-12 (probe-2 features never loaded, so V1/CA1/DG had no cell type at all; ACC labelled with the striatal four-way rule; and a plotting remap of every label >3 to 4 that erased code 5 at draw time). Every cell-type panel predating that fix is invalid.
- The 0.4 ms boundary is a fixed convention with no fitted or data-driven justification here; no bimodality check on the peak-to-trough distribution is recorded anywhere in the repo.
- The striatal rule leaves no unclassified units only if all four feature columns are finite; the binary rule is exhaustive by construction, so cortical/hippocampal "RS" is a residual category, not a positively identified class.

### Fig2C_activity_by_area_epoch_task

**Shows.** Five panels, one per recorded area (ACC, CA1, DLS, DMS, V1), each plotting mean firing rate against position along the corridor for four trial windows: trials 1-3, trials 4-10, Intermediate and Expert. Grey shading marks the landmark/visual zone and green the reward zone. The visible pattern is a broadly upward shift of the whole spatial profile from early to expert trials in ACC, DLS and CA1, a downward shift in the DMS corridor-entry transient, and little change in V1. Note that in every area the largest single step is between "Trials 1-3" and "Trials 4-10" — i.e. *within* the naive epoch, before any learning window. The panel is purely descriptive: no statistical test of any kind is run on it.

**Methods.** Input was the "supermouse" tensor `supermouse_tensor_raw` [neurons x 50 spatial bins x 30 aligned trials] built by `buildCombinedTensor.m` from each mouse's `spatial_binned_fr_all` (`buildCombinedTensor.m:128-144`), whose entries are spatially binned firing rates in Hz (spikes divided by per-bin dwell time). Bins are 4 a.u. = 5 cm, 50 bins over the 200 a.u. = 250 cm corridor (`project_cfg.m:22-26`). For task mice the trial axis carries LP-aligned windows 1:10, LP-10:LP-1 and LP+1:LP+10 written into slots 1:10, 11:20 and 21:30 (`buildCombinedTensor.m:234-236, 244-246`); the four plotted windows are slots 1:3, 4:10, 11:20 and 21:30 (`SpatioTemporalActivityEvolution.m:68`). DG units were removed (`:27-42`) and any neuron with a NaN anywhere in the tensor was dropped (`:45-46`). Aggregation is hierarchical: each mouse contributes one mean spatial profile per area and window, and the line and shaded band are the mean and SEM across mice (`:131-141`), so the inferential unit is the animal. Panel n read from the figure titles: ACC 13, CA1 2, DLS 10, DMS 13, V1 3 mice. Zone patches are drawn at bins 20-25 (visual, 80-100 a.u.) and 25-33.75 (reward, 100-135 a.u.), from `Run_TCA_pipeline.m:53-56` via `SpatioTemporalActivityEvolution.m:75-77` and `:149-150`. Source: `SpatioTemporalActivityEvolution.m:108-168`; saved by `save_to_svg` as `_Task__Raw_FR_-_Hierarchical_Spatial.svg` and collected into the manuscript set from the `save_all_open_figures` copy `figures/spatiotemporal_03_task_raw_fr_hierarchical_spatial.png`.

**Caveats.**
- Rate inflation, unfixed. Spatial firing rate is spikes divided by dwell time, and the dwell-time denominator is computed as `(bin_times(end) - bin_times(1))/1000`, i.e. (k-1)*dt for k samples rather than k*dt (`spatial_binning.m:45`; the division is at `:66`). The bias is largest where samples per bin are fewest, i.e. where the animal runs fastest, so any epoch-related change in running speed produces an apparent change in firing rate. Since behaviour does change with learning, this confounds the panel's central comparison.
- Raw, unnormalised rates. The y-axis is labelled "Raw FR" with no units (the values are Hz) and there is no per-unit normalisation, so between-epoch differences can be driven by drift, unit yield or speed rather than by tuning. Axis limits are per-area, not linked, so apparent between-area differences in the size of a shift are not comparable.
- Trial-axis gap. Slots 10 and 11 are adjacent on the tensor axis but are separated by roughly 20 real trials for a mouse at the mean LP of 41 (the true gap is per-animal, LP - 20). The panel does not show this, and the "Intermediate" window is not contiguous with the early windows.
- The largest step in every area sits between trials 1-3 and trials 4-10, both inside the naive epoch. Whatever produces it is not learning across the LP.
- No test. There is no statistic, no correction and no null on this panel; it must not be captioned as showing a significant change.
- Small and unequal n. CA1 rests on 2 mice and V1 on 3; SEM across 2 animals is not interpretable.
- The whole-tensor NaN drop (`:45-46`) removes a neuron if a single spatial bin in a single trial is NaN, which is why these n's are lower than the equivalent panels in Fig2D (ACC 13 vs 14, V1 3 vs 4). The dropped set is not random with respect to running speed.
- Different mice contribute to different areas, so cross-area comparisons of absolute rate are between different animals.

### Fig2D_neural_stability_hierarchical

**Shows.** A 6 (area, including an "ALL units" bottom row) x 3 (group) grid of single-neuron trial-to-trial reliability across the three epochs. Each coloured line is observed reliability, each grey dashed line the trial-permuted control, plotted trial by trial within Naive, Intermediate and Expert. Reliability sits above its shuffle in every task area and epoch, but it does not rise with learning: this panel is a **null result** at the animal level, and in ACC the trend is downward. The animal-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test on naive-to-expert change gives Task DMS median delta -0.0002 (n=13, p=0.735, 6 animals up / 7 down), DLS +0.0027 (n=10, p=0.557, 5 up / 5 down) and ACC -0.0259 (n=14, p=0.049, 5 up / 9 down); ACC does not survive BH correction (p_BH = 0.148 across the three primary areas).

**Methods.** Input was per-mouse spatially binned firing rate (`spatial_binned_fr_all`, 50 bins) for task and control-1, and 50 trial-duration-normalised temporal bins (`firing_rates_per_bin`) for control-2 (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:565-575`). Remaining NaNs were set to zero (`:577`), DG units dropped (`:578-580`), and each unit z-scored over its whole session (all bins x all trials, `:591-601`). The stability metric is, for each unit and each trial t, the mean of all pairwise Pearson correlations between the binned activity profiles of the trials in a 5-trial window centred on t (`window_size = 5`, `half_win = 2`, `:551-552`; computed at `:611-616` by `batch_triu_corr_mean.m` as the mean of the strictly upper triangle of the trial-by-trial correlation matrix). Unit-level curves were averaged within each mouse and area, requiring at least 5 units in that area (`min_units = 5`, `:553`, checked at `:636`), giving one value per mouse x epoch x trial-slot; the plotted line and band are the mean and SEM across mice (`:692-693`), so the inferential unit is the animal. Epoch windows: Naive = trials 1-10, Intermediate = LP-10:LP-1, Expert = LP:LP+9 (`:618-621`); controls use the rounded average task LP of 41 (`:63`, `:570`, `:573`). The grey shuffle is a **single** permutation of trial order per mouse per group, shared across all that mouse's areas (`shuff_idx = randperm(n_trials)`, `:603-604`), seeded via `rng(cfg.seed,'twister')` at `:14` since 2026-08-11 and unseeded before that date. Y-axes are linked across the whole grid and capped at 0.5 (`:655`, `:729-731`). Per-animal values are exported to `Striatum project/figures/stability_by_animal.csv` (`:1613-1634`). Panel n read from the figure: Task DMS 13, DLS 10, ACC 14, V1 4, CA1 2, ALL 14; Control-1 DMS 4, DLS 5, ACC 4, V1 3, CA1 3, ALL 5; Control-2 DMS 6, DLS 4, ACC 6, ALL 6 (V1 and CA1 read "No data"). The per-area n's are reproduced exactly by the CSV. **The reported p-values are not computed anywhere in the MATLAB pipeline** — `IntegratedAll_v1.m` contains no `signrank`/`ranksum`/`anova` call; they were computed offline from the exported CSV (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-animal naive-vs-expert reliability, animal as the unit).

**Caveats.**
- **The grey band is not a zero-information baseline.** Permuting trial order destroys temporal adjacency but leaves every trial's spatial profile intact, so the shuffle measures the session-average pairwise reliability of that unit. It sits at r ≈ 0.07-0.15 in the task areas and as low as ~0.01 in some control cells, not at 0, and the observed-minus-shuffle gap measures excess reliability of *nearby* trials over *any* trials, not tuning reliability itself.
- **The shuffle is one permutation, not a null distribution.** `cfg.n_shuffles = 25` is never read by this script (the string does not appear in it); the grey shaded band is the SEM across animals of a single permutation, not a confidence interval. No p-value can be read off the separation.
- **Window bleeding across epoch edges.** The 5-trial window is computed on the full session and only clipped at session boundaries (`:612`), so the value plotted at the last trial of an epoch draws on trials from the next epoch. At the end of the Intermediate epoch the window includes the LP trial itself.
- **The naive-to-expert comparison is refuted, not merely unproven.** In task DMS the marginal cross-animal median rises (0.221 naive to 0.259 expert), but these are the *same 13 animals* in both epochs and the paired median difference is -0.0002 — the marginal medians move only because the animals that increase do so by more than those that decrease. This is a marginal-versus-paired artefact, not a change in composition.
- BH correction is family-size dependent: ACC's p = 0.049 becomes p_BH = 0.148 over the three primary areas (DMS/DLS/ACC) and p_BH = 0.247 if all five task areas are corrected together. Neither survives.
- In control-2 DMS the observed and shuffled curves are indistinguishable in the **Intermediate** epoch (mean excess 0.002) and barely separated in Expert (0.028); in control-1 DMS and task CA1 the shuffle meets or crosses the observed line late in the Expert epoch.
- Control-1 reliability *falls* across epochs in every area (e.g. DMS -0.081, DLS -0.061, ACC -0.046, all n.s. at n = 3-5), so the epoch axis moves the metric even with nothing to learn.
- CA1 rests on 2 task mice and 3 control mice; those rows carry no inferential weight.
- Reliability is computed on z-scored rates derived from the same dwell-time denominator defect (`spatial_binning.m:45, 66`), and under-sampled bins enter as zeros rather than being excluded (`:577`), so a change in speed or speed variability across trials changes the metric independently of neural tuning.
- The "ALL units" row shown in the figure is **not in the exported CSV**: `areas`/`n_areas` are redefined to the five real areas at `:1085-1086`, before the export loop at `:1616`, so only per-area rows are written. The ALL row is therefore figure-only and cannot be re-derived from the artefact.
- No LP-shuffled null exists anywhere in the project, so the epoch structure itself is never tested against a randomly placed alignment point.

### Fig2E_position_decoding_evolution

**Shows.** Three panels (Task, Control-1 spatial, Control-2 temporal) of maximum-likelihood position decoding error against trial, plotted trial-by-trial within Naive, Intermediate and Expert, with the y-axis reversed so that up means better. Seven lines per panel: the full population, five leave-one-area-out conditions, and a tuning-shuffled control. Decoding is well below shuffle throughout in both spatial groups (task mean error 0.290 vs shuffle 0.408; control-1 0.320 vs 0.411) but **not** in control-2, where the intact decoder is no better than shuffle (0.418 vs 0.406). The improvement across epochs is a **null result** at the animal level, and control-1 improves at least as much as the task: Task median delta -0.032 (n=13, p=0.168, 8 of 13 animals improved), Control-1 -0.044 (n=5, p=0.0625), between-group Mann-Whitney p=0.924. The leave-one-area-out curves largely overlap the full-population curve, though dropping ACC or DMS does raise mean error (No-ACC 0.319, No-DMS 0.310, vs All 0.290) — no test of any condition contrast is run.

**Methods.** A Poisson maximum-likelihood decoder was run per mouse, condition and trial (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:830-871`). For each held-out trial the tuning template lambda(cell, bin) was the mean rate over **all other trials of the session** (`:840-841`, plus 1e-6); the log-likelihood of each bin was `r' * log(lambda) - sum(lambda)` (`:858`), the posterior its softmax (`:859-860`), and the predicted bin the argmax of the log-likelihood (`:863`). Trial error is root-mean-square bin error divided by the bin count, so it is expressed as a fraction of the decoded axis (`:867`); this normalisation keeps the 50-bin task/control-1 spatial axis and the 50-bin control-2 temporal axis comparable. Conditions drop all units of one area (`:818-823`) and require at least 5 remaining units (`:825`, `min_units = 5` from `:553`). The "Shuffle" condition permutes each cell's tuning curve independently across bins, redrawn for every held-out trial (`:844-848`), seeded at `:14`. NaN activity entries were set to zero before decoding (`:778`). Task trials were truncated at the disengagement point (`:763`); controls at `max_trials = 300` (`:22`, `:768`, `:773`). Epochs as in Fig2D. The plotted line and band are the mean and SEM across animals per within-epoch trial slot (`:948-951`), inferential unit = animal; y-axis reversed at `:957`, axes linked across groups at `:960`. Per-animal epoch means are exported to `Striatum project/figures/decoding_by_animal.csv` (`:1636-1657`); paired animals Task 13, Control-1 5, Control-2 6 (Task has 14 animals in Naive/Intermediate and 13 in Expert). Chance for the shuffle line is the uniform-guess value sqrt((N^2-1)/6)/N = 0.408 for N = 50, which matches the observed shuffle means (0.406-0.411 in all three groups). As for Fig2D, the p-values are **not** computed in the MATLAB script; they were obtained offline from the exported CSV. Source: `IntegratedAll_v1.m:936-961`, saved as `Decoding_Evolution_3Groups_Yoked.svg`, collected from `figures/integrated_11_ml_decoding_evolution_yoked.png`.

**Caveats.**
- **Session-global template.** The leave-one-trial-out template is built from every other trial in the session, so the naive-epoch decode uses a template partly built from expert trials. This inflates naive performance and compresses exactly the effect the panel is meant to show. It cuts both ways: task and control-1 are inflated equally, so the between-group null is not explained by it, but the within-group naive-to-expert slope is not trustworthy.
- **The improvement is not learning.** Control-1 mice, in a blank corridor with nothing to learn, improve by at least as much over epoch windows yoked to somebody else's learning point. The task-versus-control difference is p = 0.924.
- Control-1's p = 0.0625 is the **exact two-sided Wilcoxon floor at n = 5** — all five animals moved in the same direction, so this is the most extreme result obtainable at that sample size and cannot be read as "less significant than the task". The task, with n = 13, split 8 improved / 5 worsened.
- **The shuffle is a single realisation, not a null distribution.** One permutation of each cell's tuning is drawn per held-out trial; the grey band is the SEM across animals, not a confidence interval.
- Two of the six conditions are not real ablations for some groups. No-CA1 is numerically identical to All in the task (both 0.290) because CA1 exists in only 2-3 task mice, and No-V1 and No-CA1 are numerically identical to All in control-2 (all 0.418) because control-2 has probe 1 only. In the plot the last-drawn No-CA1 line occludes the All line.
- The conditions are not unit-count matched: dropping ACC removes the largest population (699 units cohort-wide) and dropping CA1 the smallest, so any separation between conditions confounds anatomical identity with population size. No test compares conditions.
- Ablation is in silico only. No physiological manipulation exists in this project; language must stay at decoder sufficiency and never at necessity.
- The x-axis within each epoch is a trial index; the visible within-epoch downward slope in the Naive block (about 0.365 falling to 0.30) is consistent with a within-session warm-up rather than an epoch effect, but nothing in the code tests this.
- Errors are normalised by axis length, which makes the task and control-2 numbers commensurable but not conceptually comparable: one is a fraction of a fixed 250 cm corridor, the other a fraction of each trial's own duration.
- No LP-shuffled null: the epoch alignment itself is never tested.

**Shows.** Decoding error as a function of position along the corridor (or of normalised time, for control-2), one panel per group, y-axis reversed, with dashed and solid vertical lines marking the visual landmark (bin 20) and reward-zone onset (bin 25) in the two spatial panels. In the task, error is lowest (about 0.05 of the axis) just before the reward line, steps up sharply immediately after bin 25, is worse at the corridor entry (about 0.17-0.24 over bins 1-10) and worst at the far end (about 0.30 by bin 50); the grey shuffle traces the opposite, inverted-U shape expected from the geometry of a bounded axis. Control-1 is far flatter and converges onto its shuffle around bins 15-30, i.e. mid-corridor position is essentially not decodable there. Control-2 runs **worse than** its shuffle from roughly bin 18 to the end of the trial.

**Methods.** Same decoder, same conditions and same normalisation as Fig2E_position_decoding_evolution, but the per-trial per-bin error `sqrt(sq_errors)/n_bins` (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:868`) is averaged over all trials of the session rather than split by epoch (`:873`), giving one error-versus-bin profile per animal, condition and group. The plotted line and band are the mean and SEM across animals (`:977-983`), inferential unit = animal (Task 14, Control-1 5, Control-2 6). Landmark lines are drawn only for the two spatial groups (`:989-996`) at `visual_zone_cm/bin_size_au` = 80/4 = bin 20 and `reward_zone_cm/bin_size_au` = 100/4 = bin 25 (`:991-992`, values from `project_cfg.m:35-36`; the field names say cm but the values are a.u., equivalent to 100 cm and 125 cm), each rescaled by `n_bins_g/n_bins`, which is 1 at the current 50-bin grid. Control-2 is plotted against temporal bin, since its 50 bins are equal fractions of each trial's own duration (`PreprocessStriatumControl2.m:6, 74`), so bin width varies trial to trial. Axes linked across groups (`:1003`). Source: `IntegratedAll_v1.m:963-1004`, saved as `Decoding_Spatial_Bin_Error_3Groups.svg`, collected from `figures/integrated_12_spatial_decoding_error_profile_across_corridor.png`. **The per-bin profile is not exported to any CSV** — the values quoted above are read off the figure, not from an artefact.

**Caveats.**
- **The profile shape is dominated by axis geometry, not by information.** On a bounded axis a random guess is closest to the truth at the centre, which is exactly the inverted-U the grey shuffle shows (about 0.49 at bin 1, 0.25 at bin 25, 0.48-0.50 at bin 50). The coloured curves must therefore be read against the grey curve at each bin, never in absolute terms; the raw dip at bins 20-25 is not by itself evidence of enhanced coding near the landmark.
- Control-1 coloured curves meet the shuffle around bins 15-30, and control-2 coloured curves run *worse* than the shuffle from about bin 18 onward. In control-2 the decoder is therefore worse than a scrambled template over most of the trial, which is a clean statement that there is nothing temporal to decode there, not a positive result. The epoch-level export corroborates it (control-2 All 0.418 vs Shuffle 0.406).
- The step at the reward line (bin 25) coincides with where mice slow or stop, so spike counts per bin rise sharply there. Given the dwell-time denominator defect (`spatial_binning.m:45, 66`), part of that step is a rate-estimation artefact rather than a change in coding.
- The profile collapses all trials of the session, so it says nothing about learning and must not be captioned as an epoch result.
- Leave-one-area-out conditions are not unit-count matched, and No-CA1 (task) / No-V1 and No-CA1 (control-2) are not real ablations, as above.
- Bins sampled by fewer than two position samples are left NaN at binning time (`spatial_binning.m:39`), but they are **not excluded** downstream — `activity(isnan(activity)) = 0` (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:778`) enters them into the decoder as zero-rate bins. Fast-traversed bins therefore contribute silent, uninformative data rather than being dropped.

### Fig2E_decoding_certainty_profile

**Shows.** Normalised posterior entropy (H / log2 N) against corridor position, one panel per group, y-axis reversed so that up means more certain, with a dotted reference at 1.0 marking a uniform posterior (chance). All conditions sit far from chance (roughly 0.08-0.30 in the two spatial groups; the No-ACC condition in control-2 reaches about 0.46), i.e. very peaked posteriors. Entropy is at its minimum just before the reward line and steps up immediately after bin 25. The important and uncomfortable observation is that the grey tuning-shuffled condition is as certain as, or **more certain than**, the intact decoder throughout (task mean normalised entropy: Shuffle 0.094 vs All 0.129). Entropy therefore does not track decoding accuracy in this analysis, and the epoch-wise version of the same metric moves the wrong way for a sharpening account: normalised entropy **rises** naive to expert (Task median 0.073 to 0.097, median paired delta +0.012, p = 0.021, 10 of 13 animals up).

**Methods.** Per bin b of each held-out trial the decoder's posterior over bins was computed as in Fig2E_position_decoding_evolution, its Shannon entropy taken in bits (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:862`) and divided by log2(n_bins) (`:869-870`), so 1.0 is exactly a uniform posterior over the 50 bins and 0 a delta function. Per-bin entropies were averaged over all trials of the session (`:874`), then across animals with SEM bands (`:1020-1026`), inferential unit = animal (Task 14, Control-1 5, Control-2 6). The chance reference line at 1.0 was added on 2026-08-11 (`:1045`); the y-axis is reversed (`:1046`) and linked across groups (`:1049`). Landmark lines as in the error profile, again only for the two spatial groups (`:1032-1038`). Epoch-wise entropy per animal is in `figures/decoding_by_animal.csv`, column `norm_entropy`; the epoch p-values were computed offline from that file (paired Wilcoxon, animal as unit), not in the MATLAB script. Source: `IntegratedAll_v1.m:1006-1050`, saved as `Decoding_Spatial_Entropy_Bin_Profile_3Groups.svg`, collected from `figures/integrated_13_spatial_certainty_profile_across_corridor.png`.

**Caveats.**
- **Entropy is not a validity measure here.** The shuffled-template condition produces posteriors at least as peaked as the intact one, plausibly because independently permuting each cell's tuning removes the mutual consistency that spreads probability over neighbouring bins. Certainty and correctness are dissociated in this panel and it must not be captioned as "the code sharpens".
- The secondary epoch result contradicts the sharpening narrative outright: the posterior gets *flatter* with learning in the task (median delta +0.012, p = 0.021, 10/13 animals up) and in control-2 (p = 0.031, 6/6 up). Control-1 shows no change (p = 0.438), so the effect is not simply a yoked-time artefact — but it is the opposite of the predicted direction.
- The Poisson log-likelihood scales with spike count, so any bin where the animal dwells longer yields a peakier posterior irrespective of information. The certainty step at the reward line coincides with where mice slow, and the shuffle shows a similar profile, which points at count magnitude rather than coding. The dwell-time denominator defect (`spatial_binning.m:45, 66`) sits directly on this axis.
- Between conditions, entropy tracks the number of contributing units: No-ACC is the least certain in the task (0.205 vs All 0.129) and in control-2 (0.380 vs 0.184), and No-DLS in control-1 (0.225 vs 0.124) — in each case the condition removing the most units for that group. Without unit-count matching, condition ordering is uninterpretable as an anatomical result.
- Averaged over all trials of the session, so this panel carries no epoch information; the epoch claim comes from the CSV, not from the figure. The per-bin profile itself is not exported.
- Control-2's 50 temporal bins are fractions of each trial's own duration, so "position" on its x-axis is not a fixed physical quantity, and its No-V1/No-CA1 conditions are identical to All (no probe-2 units).
