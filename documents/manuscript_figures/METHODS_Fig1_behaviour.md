## Figure 1 — behaviour and learning

*Conventions used throughout this cluster.* All spatial panels used the 5 cm binning standardised on 2026-08-10: bin width 4 a.u. = 5 cm, 50 bins spanning the 200 a.u. (250 cm) corridor (`project_cfg.m:22-26`, `ProcessStriatumTask.m:18-22`). The visual landmark lay at 80 a.u. (100 cm, bin 20) and the reward zone at 100–135 a.u. (125–169 cm, bins 25–33.75) (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:11-13`). Note that the config fields carrying these landmark positions are misnamed: `cfg.visual_zone_cm = 80` and `cfg.reward_zone_cm = 100` hold **a.u.**, not cm (`project_cfg.m:35-36`); every consumer divides them by `bin_size_au`, so the drawn bins (20, 25) are correct, but the names are a trap. Where a mean ± SEM band is drawn across animals, the **animal-level SEM convention** was: each animal contributed one value per bin (or per within-epoch trial slot), the band was `std(X,0,1,'omitnan') ./ sqrt(sum(~isnan(X),1))`, so the denominator is the number of animals contributing a finite value *at that x-position* and may vary along the abscissa (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:277, 388, 407, 500, 523` — all five verified). The learning point (LP) rule was: on the per-trial z-scored lick error, the first trial that was itself ≤ −2 **and** whose forward window of 10 trials contained ≥ 7 sub-threshold trials (`find_learning_points.m:40-50`; parameters `lp_z_threshold = -2`, `lp_window = 10`, `lp_min_consecutive = 7`, `project_cfg.m:65-67`). Recomputing this rule directly from `processed_data/preprocessed_data5cm.mat` returned LPs of 22, 44, 32, 53, 54, 36, 33, 14, 44, 67, 84, NaN, 23, 39, 26, NaN — 14 learners of 16 task mice, mean LP 40.79, rounded to 41 for control yoking (verified by recomputation). Epochs were Naive = trials 1–10, Intermediate = `lp-10 : lp-1`, Expert = `lp : lp+9` (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:253-256`), i.e. **Expert includes the LP trial**. The shared helper `epoch_indices.m` parameterises both conventions via `expert_starts_at` ('lp' vs 'lp1'; `epoch_indices.m:47-55`), and `project_cfg.m:68` sets the default to `'lp'`; the legacy `processTaskData.m:80` path and the MI/decoding scripts (`MutualInformationStriatum_v2.m:1055`, `Nonlinear_Epoch_Decoding.m:80`, `CCA_striatum_spatial_v3.m:283`) use `lp+1 : lp+10`. Controls have no LP and were yoked to the task mean, LP = 41 (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:322, 462`).

### Fig1B_lick_heatmap_task

**Shows.** A tiled grid (`tiledlayout('flow')`, 16 tiles), one per task mouse, each showing lick rate as a heat map with trials on the ordinate (71–501 rows, mouse-dependent) and the 50 spatial bins on the abscissa; colour is lick rate in Hz. A dashed white line marks the visual landmark (bin 20) and a solid white line the reward-zone start (bin 25). The reader sees that in early trials licking is smeared across the whole corridor, and that over trials it condenses into a narrow band at the reward-zone entrance in most animals, with the post-reward corridor going quiet. This is the qualitative version of the quantified epoch profiles in Fig1C_behavioural_evolution_yoked.

**Methods.** Per trial and bin, lick rate = `spatial_binned_data.licks ./ spatial_binned_data.durations`, i.e. lick count divided by occupancy in seconds (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:141-143`). Licks and occupancy were computed at preprocessing by binning the corridor position trace into edges `0:4:200` with the final edge extended by one bin width, summing licks per bin and taking occupancy as the elapsed time between the first and last position sample in the bin, divided by 1000 (ms → s) (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:18-22`, `spatial_binning.m:30, 41, 45`). Bins containing ≤ 1 position sample were left NaN (`spatial_binning.m:39`). Before display each animal's rate matrix was capped at its own 99th percentile (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:148-149`) and drawn with `imagesc`. n = 16 mice, one session each; no averaging, no statistic, and no inferential test is computed for this panel. Source: `/Users/theoamvr/Desktop/Experiments/StriatumACC/Striatum project/IntegratedAll_v1.m:129-167`; figure file `figures/integrated_01_task_spatial_lick_rate_heatmaps.png`.

**Caveats.**
- **Occupancy-denominator defect (unfixed).** Bin occupancy is `bin_times(end) - bin_times(1)`, i.e. (k−1)·dt rather than k·dt for k samples (`spatial_binning.m:45`). Rates are therefore inflated by a factor k/(k−1), and the inflation is speed-dependent: because mice run faster with learning (verified below in Fig1C), the inflation itself changes across the very axis the panel is used to argue about. Widening bins from 2.5 to 5 cm roughly doubles k and so roughly halves the relative inflation — this is an analytic expectation from the estimator, **not** a measured quantity, and it does not remove the speed dependence. No speed-matched control exists in the repository.
- **The colourbar describes one tile only.** `colorbar(ax)` is attached to the last tile created (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:165`, `ax` being Mouse 16's axes) while each `imagesc` autoscales to its own capped data, so the single "Lick Rate (Hz)" scale describes Mouse 16 alone. Combined with the per-animal 99th-percentile cap, colour is not comparable across tiles.
- Only the reward-zone **start** is drawn; the reward-zone end (135 a.u., bin 33.75) is not marked, so the panel understates the zone's extent.
- Rows are consecutive *retained* trials after the empty-reward exclusion and the final-trial drop (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:88, 94-95`), not original trial numbers; the trial axis is therefore not strictly the session's trial index.
- Session lengths differ by a factor of seven across mice (71 to 501), so the same vertical distance means very different amounts of experience in different tiles.
- **Do not caption the two non-learners as showing "no band".** Mice 12 and 16 are the two animals for which the LP rule never fires, but the LP rule is a criterion on the z-scored lick error, not on the heat map: recomputing mid-late 10-trial lick profiles gives Mouse 12 a peak of 5.5 Hz at bin 22 (immediately pre-reward) and Mouse 16 a peak of 2.3 Hz at bin 28. Any claim that a particular tile lacks the band is an eyeball claim and is contradicted for Mouse 12.

### Fig1B_performance_zerror_task

**Shows.** Sixteen tiles, one per task mouse, of the smoothed z-scored lick error against trial number: black line with a grey band, a red dashed line at z = 0 and a blue dashed line at the LP threshold z = −2. A green vertical line marks each animal's LP. The reader sees performance fall below chance over tens of trials in 14 of 16 animals; the two tiles with no green line (Mouse 12 and Mouse 16) are the animals for which the LP rule never fires. Y-axes are per-tile, not linked.

**Methods.** Lick precision was computed per trial at preprocessing (`calculate_lick_precision.m`): licks beyond the reward-zone start were discarded (`l(l>rz_start) = nan`), and the raw error was the sum of squared distances of the remaining raw lick positions from the reward-zone start, which is passed in as 100 a.u. (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:120-121`), `p = sum((l - rz_start).^2, 2, 'omitmissing')` (`calculate_lick_precision.m:11-12`). A per-trial null was built from **1000 uniform shuffles**: `rz_start .* rand(1000, lick_number)`, i.e. the same number of licks redrawn uniformly over 0–100 a.u., with the same sum-of-squares statistic per shuffle (`calculate_lick_precision.m:21-22`). The z-scored lick error is `(raw error − shuffle mean) / shuffle SD` per trial (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:166`). The first trial of every session and trials flagged by `isoutlier(..., "percentiles", [0,99])` were set to NaN before z-scoring (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:161-165`). The shuffle RNG was seeded `rng(42,'twister')` (`ProcessStriatumTask.m:8`). For display, each animal's trace was smoothed with a 10-trial moving mean (`mov_avg_window = 10`, `IntegratedAll_v1.m:21`) and the band is a 10-trial moving SD divided by √10; points where the smoothed value exceeded 10 were blanked (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:179-183`). n = 16 mice; LPs from `find_learning_points.m` as above. Source `IntegratedAll_v1.m:169-196`; figure file `figures/integrated_04_task_group_z_scored_lick_errors.png`.

**Caveats.**
- The grey band is a **within-animal moving SEM over trials**, not an animal-level SEM; it is a smoothing artefact of the 10-trial window, and adjacent points are not independent.
- The LP was detected on the **raw** z-error, whereas the plotted trace is smoothed, so the green LP line can sit where the smoothed line has not yet crossed −2.
- The shuffle null is uniform over 0–100 a.u. and conditions only on the number of licks; it does not control for run speed, lick-bout structure, or occupancy, so z = −2 is a statement about spatial concentration relative to a uniform licker, not about task performance per se.
- The blanking rule (smoothed z > 10) produces genuine gaps in **two** traces, Mouse 5 (6 blanked points, peak smoothed z = 20.5) and Mouse 11 (1 point, 11.9), recomputed from the cache with the MATLAB even-window `movmean` convention. Those are missing data, not zeros.
- Per-tile y-limits differ by roughly an order of magnitude; the panel cannot be read as a magnitude comparison across mice.
- No statistical test is performed here; the two non-learners are defined solely by the LP rule failing to fire.
- Any claim that traces "drift back up late in the session" is an eyeball reading of the tiles and is not quantified anywhere in this script; the repository's disengagement estimate is a separate quantity (`change_point_mean`), used by the RL fits but not plotted here.

### Fig1C_behavioural_stability_epochs

**Shows.** A 2 × 3 grid. Top row: trial-to-trial stability of the spatial (or temporal) lick pattern for Task learners, Control-1 and Control-2, plotted as mean ± SEM Pearson r across the 30 epoch-aligned trial slots, y-axes linked across the three groups. Bottom row: the same for the velocity pattern (Task, Control-1; the Control-2 tile is blanked, "No Velocity (Wheel Blocked)"). Recomputed epoch means: task lick stability rises **0.095 → 0.178 → 0.289** and task velocity stability rises **0.354 → 0.382 → 0.424**; control-1 lick stability is near zero throughout with a small mid-session bump (0.010 → 0.071 → 0.039), control-1 velocity stability is flat at ≈ 0 (−0.002 → 0.016 → 0.006), and control-2 lick stability is flat at ≈ 0.18–0.21 across the yoked epochs (0.180 → 0.206 → 0.183). **No test is computed for any of this**; the apparent task rise is an unquantified visual impression.

**Methods.** For each animal and each trial t, a sliding window of `window_size_beh = 5` trials (`half_win_beh = floor(5/2) = 2`, i.e. t−2 : t+2, truncated at session edges) was taken; the trials in the window were correlated with one another across bins and the mean of the upper triangle of the resulting Pearson correlation matrix was the stability value at trial t (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:305-306, 345-353`, helper `calc_triu_corr` at `IntegratedAll_v1.m:1697-1704`). Inputs were the lick rate `licks ./ durations` and velocity `= (cfg.bin_size_au × 1.25) ./ durations = 5 cm / durations` in cm/s (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:326-338`); Control-2 used the trial-normalised temporal lick rates with a unit-duration denominator and has no velocity (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:331-335`). The continuous stability trace was then sliced into the three epochs with the same index rule as Fig1B_performance_by_epoch and plotted as animal-level mean ± SEM per trial slot (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:356-370, 386-390, 405-409`). Task used learners only (non-learners `continue`, `IntegratedAll_v1.m:316-320`): n = 14. Controls were yoked to LP = 41: n = 5 (Control-1), n = 6 (Control-2). All the numbers quoted above were recomputed independently from the three caches, reproducing the script's arithmetic. Source `IntegratedAll_v1.m:303-427`; figure file `figures/integrated_07_behavioral_stability_evolution_epochs.png`.

**Caveats.**
- **No statistical test is computed for this panel anywhere in the script** (verified by grep: `IntegratedAll_v1.m` contains no `signrank`, `ranksum`, `anova` or `ttest`). Its only inferential exports are the neural stability and decoding CSVs (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:1632, 1655`). Treat the apparent rise exactly as the neural stability claim was treated once quantified (refuted) until an animal-level paired test has been run.
- The 5-trial sliding window makes neighbouring trial slots strongly dependent and **bleeds across epoch boundaries**: the value at Naive slot 10 is computed from trials 8–12, and the value at Expert slot 1 (the LP trial) is computed from trials LP−2 : LP+2, i.e. partly from pre-LP trials. Epoch means are therefore not independent samples of the epochs.
- Lick rate and velocity both carry the (k−1)·dt occupancy defect (`spatial_binning.m:45`), and both are ratios of the *same* duration term, so the two rows are not independent measures.
- Speed itself changes with learning (verified in Fig1C_behavioural_evolution_yoked: 26 → 37 cm/s in the pre-landmark corridor), so a rise in velocity-profile stability can reflect a change in the running-speed distribution rather than in behavioural reproducibility; there is no speed-matched control.
- Control-1 (n = 5) and Control-2 (n = 6) give SEM bands that are wide and uninformative; the `linkaxes` y-linking (`IntegratedAll_v1.m:425-426`) makes them look comparable to the task band, which they are not.
- Control-2 stability is measured over trial-normalised temporal bins, a different quantity from the spatial correlations in the other two columns; the shared y-axis invites an apples-to-oranges reading.
- Slot-wise n in the task column is 13 rather than 14 at some slots (an animal's window can be all-NaN); the SEM denominator therefore varies along the abscissa.

### Fig1E_RL_lick_profiles

**Caveats.**
- **The LL-gain numbers in the tile titles must not be quoted.** They are `model_lick_test − null_lick_test` (`plot_real_data.py:109`) against a saturated per-bin null whose lick term is `Poisson(λ_b)` with `λ_b` the mean training lick *count* in bin b — with **no dwell-time exposure term** (`fit_real_data.py:103-124`, especially `:117, :120-121`) — while the model is scored as `Poisson(λ_rate · dt)` with `dt` from the observed velocity. The model is therefore handed an occupancy regressor the null is denied. Recomputing across the 16 `.npz` files gives a **mean gain of +0.604 nats/bin, positive in 16/16 mice** (this is a mean, not a sum). An exposure-matched null collapses it: `documents/MANUSCRIPT_INVENTORY.md:4937` records +0.604 → **+0.109** nats/bin (16/16 → 11/16 mice positive) for interleaved CV and +0.454 → +0.091 (15/16 → 10/16) for forward CV, while `documents/FIGURE_PLAN_AUDIT.md:70-73` records a second independent specification giving ≈ +0.36 (12/16). Direction confirmed twice, magnitude specification-dependent, and **neither collapse number has a backing script in the repository** (verified: no script under `rl_model/scripts/` computes an exposure-matched null).
- **A second, separate asymmetry favours the model.** Because `learn_mask` includes the held-out trials, the model's prediction at bin b of a held-out trial is conditioned on that same trial's earlier bins, whereas the null has no within-trial adaptation (`documents/MANUSCRIPT_INVENTORY.md:4993`). This is a one-step-ahead/filtering likelihood, not a trial-level hold-out, and must be described as such if any CV number is reported.
- **The citable RL-behaviour result is the per-epoch reproduction, not this panel.** Recomputed directly from `real_fits_v6_interleaved` plus the cache: the Naive→Expert lick-profile change is reproduced (Δr > 0.5) for **11 of the 13 evaluable mice, mean Δr +0.702**, where Δr is the correlation between the observed and modelled Expert-minus-Naive profile difference (`scripts/plot_epoch_validation.py:118-127`). The three unevaluable mice are M12 and M16 (no LP) and M15 (LP 26 but only 27 fitted trials, so `lp+10 > n_trials`). Be explicit about the denominator: the figure's own suptitle prints **11/16**, because `len(rs)` counts all 16 mice including the three that yield NaN (`plot_epoch_validation.py:135-140`). The velocity counterpart is 6/16 (6 of the same 13 evaluable), mean Δr **+0.355**, and the held-out velocity CV gain is **mean −0.064 nats/bin, positive in only 3/16 mice** — marginally negative, not "near zero".
- **This is the demoted figure.** The design document states explicitly that the session-averaged `_profile()` figures were demoted because a session average hides exactly the failure the redesign targets (`UNDERSTANDING.md:107-112`); the intended headline validation is per-epoch. Using this panel as Fig 1E shows the model matching the time-averaged reward-zone peak, which the pre-redesign model with frozen latents also did.
- The velocity channel — the model's second emission — is not shown at all, and it is the weak channel.
- Sessions were truncated at the disengagement change point, so the tiles cover 27–225 trials of 71–501-trial sessions; the profiles describe the engaged portion only, and M15 (27 trials) is fitted on very little data.
- Observed lick counts and the `dt` the model conditions on both come from the defective occupancy denominator (`spatial_binning.m:45`), so the same speed-dependent bias is present in both the data and the model's exposure term.

- Descriptions of which tiles show idiosyncratic off-reward spikes are eyeball readings of the rendered figure and were not recomputed; do not put specific bin indices in a caption without re-deriving them.
