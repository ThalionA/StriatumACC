### Fig3A_TCA_rank_diagnostics

**Shows.** Two panels of rank-selection diagnostics for the combined decomposition: BIC against rank 2–8 (left) and relative reconstruction error against rank (right). BIC falls steeply from rank 2, reaches its minimum at rank 4, and rises thereafter; reconstruction error falls monotonically but with steadily shrinking increments (−0.0083 from rank 2→3, down to −0.0025 from 7→8), so it offers no sharp elbow. The left panel is annotated with all three candidate answers the pipeline produced — "tolerance-selected (3)", "BIC minimum (4)" and, in orange with a square marker, "hardcoded override (5)" — and the suptitle carries the caveat that BIC here treats every tensor entry as an independent observation. The panel is, in effect, the evidence that the rank used in the rest of Figure 3 was not chosen by the stated criterion.

**Methods.** BIC and relative reconstruction error were computed inside `tca_with_bic_extended.m` for the best of 25 initialisations at each rank 2–8 on the 1140 × 50 × 30 tensor. BIC = n·log(RSS/n) + p·log(n) with n = numel(tensor) = 1,710,000 and p = R·(I+J+K) = R·1220 (`tca_with_bic_extended.m:92-101`). Values (rank: BIC, relative error, mean initialisation similarity): 2: −7,579,838.27, 0.4473, 1.000; 3: −7,594,510.59, 0.4389, 0.850; 4: −7,595,647.07, 0.4342, 0.882; 5: −7,593,439.88, 0.4303, 0.826; 6: −7,588,711.23, 0.4271, 0.822; 7: −7,583,000.90, 0.4241, 0.806; 8: −7,575,691.34, 0.4216, 0.804 (`processed_data/tca_rerun_2026-08-11.log`, reproduced digit-for-digit in `regen_chain2_2026-08-11.log`). The selection rule takes the smallest rank whose BIC lies within a relative tolerance of 0.1% of the minimum (`tol = 0.001`; `bic_threshold = min_bic + |min_bic|·tol`, `tca_with_bic_extended.m:119-132`); the threshold was −7,588,051.42, which admitted ranks 3–6 and returned 3. The pipeline then discards that result at `Run_TCA_pipeline.m:1042`. The panel plotted here is an ad-hoc matplotlib rendering of the logged values (session scratchpad `plot_tca_factors.py`), which hardcodes the ranks-2–8 BIC and reconstruction-error arrays from the run log.

**Caveats.**
- **BIC is not a valid model-selection criterion on this tensor.** It counts all 1,710,000 entries as independent observations, whereas neighbouring spatial bins and consecutive trials are strongly autocorrelated; the effective sample size is far smaller, so the penalty term p·log(n) is on an arbitrary scale. The whole BIC range across ranks 2–8 spans 0.26% of the statistic's magnitude, and the rank-4→5 comparison is a near-cancellation of a −15,303 fit term against a +17,510 penalty term (net ΔBIC = +2,207). The panel's suptitle states this caveat; a caption must not launder it.
- The BIC at each rank is computed from the *minimum* reconstruction error over 25 unseeded initialisations, i.e. from an order statistic rather than a likelihood, which biases the fit term downward by an amount that varies with rank.
- **The pipeline's own diagnostics figure carries a false label.** After the override, `Run_TCA_pipeline.m:1103` redraws the same curves with `xline(best_n_factors, ..., 'Selected = %d')`, producing `figures/tca_11_fig.png` annotated "Selected = 5" at a rank no criterion selected (verified by inspecting the PNG; the pre-override twin, `tca_10_fig.png`, is annotated "Selected = 3"). Neither of those is the panel collected here; the collected panel is the honest ad-hoc rendering. Any figure set assembled from the MATLAB sweep rather than from this file will silently reintroduce the false statement.
- The panel drops the initialisation-similarity curve that the MATLAB figures carry. That curve is **not** monotonic — 1.000, 0.850, 0.882, 0.826, 0.822, 0.806, 0.804 for ranks 2–8 — it dips at rank 3 and rebounds at rank 4. Its highest non-trivial value therefore falls at the BIC minimum, so this diagnostic weakly favours rank 4 over rank 5 rather than arguing generically for a lower rank.
- Rendering defect: all three annotations ("tolerance-selected (3)", "BIC minimum (4)", "hardcoded override (5)") sit at the bottom of the left panel and collide with the x-axis label, which is illegible in the current render.

### Fig3B_TCA_task_vs_control_trialfactors

**Shows.** The decisive negative control for the whole TCA arm. Two panels of trial factors, each normalised to its own maximum absolute value and plotted across the 30 aligned slots with the Naive and Post-LP blocks shaded: left, the five components of a task-mice-only decomposition; right, the four components of a control-mice-only decomposition. **The control mice reproduce the task pattern.** In the task decomposition the three largest slot-10→11 steps are −0.60, +0.61 and +0.34 in the plotted (max-|value|-normalised) units; in the control decomposition they are −0.57, +0.59 and +0.38. In both, the steps at the slot-20|21 boundary — the LP itself — are far smaller: the largest is 0.14 in the task (about four-fold smaller than the largest 10|11 step) and 0.04 in the control (more than an order of magnitude smaller). Control mice never learn anything and have no learning point, so a step of this size and sign structure cannot be a signature of learning; it is what the ~20-trial gap built into the slot axis at the 10|11 boundary produces on its own. Trial-factor steps in Fig3A are therefore not evidence of ensemble emergence around the learning point.

**Methods.** Two decompositions were run by setting the `TCA_MODE` environment variable (`Run_TCA_pipeline.m:24-26`), each with per-area balancing switched off (`TCA_UNBALANCED=1`, `Run_TCA_pipeline.m:50`) so that every NaN-free unit entered. Task-only: 14 task mice entered the tensor build (1679 units), 63 units (3.8%) were dropped as NaN, leaving **1616 units from 13 animals** (ACC 568, DLS 502, DMS 339, V1 155, CA1 52) × 50 bins × 30 slots — one task animal contributes no NaN-free unit at all (log: "13 animals present in valid labels, tensor_info reported 14 total"). Its BIC minimum was rank 5 (−11,165,323.50) and the tolerance rule returned 4, so the rank-5 model is plotted. Control-only: 5 control-1 mice entered (1041 units), 192 (18.4%) were dropped as NaN, leaving **849 units from 4 animals** (ACC 244, V1 191, DLS 189, CA1 176, DMS 49); its BIC minimum and tolerance selection both returned rank 2, and rank 4 is plotted deliberately over-fitted so that the control is given at least as many components as the task in which to express a step (log `processed_data/tca_pergroup_2026-08-11.log`; caches `tca_outputs_task_unbalanced.mat`, `tca_outputs_control_unbalanced.mat`). All other settings were identical to the combined run (`cp_nmu`, min–max per unit, 25 initialisations, 200 iterations, ranks 2–8). Control slots were fixed by yoking to the mean task LP of 41: slots 1:10 = trials 1–10, 11:20 = trials 31–40, 21:30 = trials 42–51 (printed in the run log; `buildCombinedTensor.m:264-266`), i.e. exactly the same ~20-trial discontinuity at the 10|11 boundary as the task alignment produces. Each trial factor was divided by its own maximum absolute value before plotting (scratchpad `plot_pergroup.py`), so the panels compare shape, not amplitude. Step sizes quoted above were recomputed from the exported factor matrices in the same normalisation the panel uses.

**Caveats.**
- The comparison establishes only that the slot-boundary step arises *without* learning. It does not license the converse claim that task and control components are the same object: the two tensors have very different anatomical composition (task striatum-heavy, 52 CA1 units; control CA1/V1-heavy, 49 DMS units) and different unit counts, and no component-matching statistic was computed between them.
- No inferential test is attached to this panel. The step sizes are descriptive quantities read from single decompositions with no error bars, no per-animal resampling and no null distribution.
- The two decompositions rest on 13 and 4 animals respectively — the control arm in particular is four mice — and are unit-pooled with no animal-level structure.
- Control-1 is a blank-corridor habituation group, i.e. a no-task baseline, not a yoked control for learning; the argument here is a demonstration of an artefact, not a matched contrast.
- The control decomposition is plotted at rank 4 against its own BIC-selected rank of 2, and the task panel's title reads "BIC min", which is true of that decomposition (rank 5) but is easily misread as endorsing rank 5 in the *combined* decomposition of Fig3A, where 5 was the override.
- Per-group decompositions were unbalanced, so area contributions are proportional to yield and ACC dominates the task tensor; Fig3A's components are not directly comparable to either panel here.
- The same occupancy-denominator defect (`spatial_binning.m:45`) and the absence of any LP-shuffled null apply.

### Fig3C_ensemble_area_type_composition

**Shows.** The joint breakdown: one grouped bar chart per ensemble, area on the x-axis (ACC, CA1, DLS, DMS, V1) and three bars per area (MSN, FSN, TAN) expressed as a percentage of that ensemble's total units. Read at face value, the panel says every ensemble's blue (MSN) mass sits exclusively in DLS and DMS, and that ACC, CA1 and V1 contribute only orange (FSN) bars — i.e. that cortex and hippocampus contain fast-spiking interneurons and no principal cells. **That is an artefact of the type map, not a result.** The genuine content is the striatal split: ensemble 4 is DLS-MSN-dominated (27.7% of its units), ensembles 1 and 5 are DMS-MSN-dominated (22.9% and 20.7%), and TAN bars appear only in DLS/DMS at ≤ 2.9%.

**Methods.** Same assignments and same type labels as the two panels above, but computed over **all** units in each ensemble rather than pure units only (`ensemble_analysis.m:304-328`): for each ensemble, the count of units matching each (area, type) pair divided by the ensemble's total unit count. Types were again restricted to codes 1–3 by `neurontype_map` (defined at `ensemble_analysis.m:97`, re-read at `:295-297`). Axes are linked across subplots (`ensemble_analysis.m:359`), so heights are comparable panel to panel; the common y-limit is 0–30%, and the tiled layout carries a shared y-label, "Proportion of Neurons in Ensemble (%)" (`:363`) — both confirmed from the SVG tick labels. Figure emitted 2026-08-12 00:28 as `figures/ensemble_09_joint_area_and_neuron_type_composition_of_ensembles.png/.svg`.

**Caveats.**
- **The empty MSN bars over ACC, CA1 and V1 are a coding artefact.** Those areas' principal cells carry type code 5 (RS), which the map omits; the per-area type census in the tensor is ACC 193 RS + 35 FS, V1 203 RS + 25 FS, CA1 212 RS + 16 FS, against DMS 203 MSN / 12 FSN / 9 TAN / 4 UIN and DLS 188 / 26 / 13 / 1. As drawn, the panel is affirmatively misleading about cortical and hippocampal composition and must not be published in this form.
- Because the omitted RS units remain in each ensemble's denominator, all bars are deflated by roughly the RS share (~53%) and the plotted percentages do not sum to 100% within an ensemble.
- This panel uses all units while the sibling type panel uses only pure units, so the two `Fig3C` type panels are not mutually consistent.
- No statistical test of any kind is applied; there is no per-animal aggregation, no null and no correction.
- All upstream caveats of the tensor apply (unseeded balanced subsample, 41% control units, occupancy-denominator defect, arbitrary ensemble numbering).

### Fig3D_ensemble_ablation_delta

**Shows.** In-silico ablation: box plots of the change in absolute position-decoding error, per aligned trial, when each ensemble in turn is removed from the decoder, relative to the full 1140-unit decoder (dashed line at zero). Two ensembles carry the position signal: knocking out ensemble 4 costs a median +1.30 bins (6.5 cm) and ensemble 5 +1.20 bins (6.0 cm), both marked ***. Ensembles 1 and 3 cost nothing (medians +0.07 and +0.12, n.s.), and ensemble 2 is marked * for a *negative* median (−0.11 bins) — removing it slightly improves decoding. The two costly ensembles are precisely the two with sharp, localised spatial factors in Fig3A (components 4 and 5, peaks at 102.5 cm and 7.5 cm), while the ensembles that cost nothing are the broad or nearly untuned components. The panel supports a sufficiency claim about a subset of components, not a necessity claim about any circuit.

**Methods.** Decoding used `decode_ensemble_ablation.m` on the raw-rate balanced tensor `supermouse_combined_valid` (1140 units × 50 bins × 30 slots; values are firing rates in Hz, range 0–201.91 Hz, verified from the cache), with ensemble assignments as above (`ensemble_analysis.m:1016-1017`). For each held-out trial the spatial template was the mean rate profile over the other 29 trials (`:57-74`, template at `:67`), and each test bin was assigned to the bin maximising the Poisson-form log-likelihood Σ_n [r_n·log max(λ_n,1e-6) − λ_n] (`:55,68-72`). Error is the absolute difference between predicted and true bin index, in 5 cm bins, averaged across the 50 bins within each trial to give 30 values per decoder (`:76-81`). The knock-out decoder for ensemble *e* used all units with assignment ≠ *e* (`:117-119`). Significance is a one-sample t-test of the 30 per-trial deltas against zero (`ensemble_analysis.m:1079-1080`), with stars at p < 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.001 (`:1086`). Independently reimplemented values (median delta, t, p, n units): ensemble 1 +0.070, t = −0.68, p = 0.50, 236 units; ensemble 2 −0.110, t = −2.23, p = 0.034, 267; ensemble 3 +0.120, t = +0.88, p = 0.39, 175; ensemble 4 +1.300, t = +6.64, p = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷, 206; ensemble 5 +1.200, t = +5.97, p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁶, 256. The reimplementation reproduced the pipeline's baseline RMSE (8.3025 vs the printed 8.30) and the baseline per-trial median error (3.83 bins). Figure emitted 2026-08-12 00:29 as `figures/ensemble_58_decoding_ko_delta.png/.svg`.

**Caveats.**
- **Leak history.** Until 2026-08-11 this decoder trained unconditionally on trials 21:30, so post-LP test trials sat inside their own template while naive trials were decoded with an expert template; the leakage and the expected learning effect pointed the same way. The leave-one-trial-out template was restored at `decode_ensemble_ablation.m:62-67` (commit `f3b871e`, 11 Aug 16:46), and only figures postdating that commit are trustworthy. This panel (12 Aug 00:29) does postdate it.
- The restored template is still **session-global**: the leave-one-out set spans all 30 slots, so naive-epoch trials are decoded with a template built largely from intermediate and expert trials. Any epoch-resolved reading of this panel inherits that defect (the same class as the Fig2E decoder).
- **The inferential unit is the trial slot of a pooled supermouse, n = 30**, not the animal — the code says so explicitly ("supermouse: no animal-level unit exists"). The 30 slots are not independent (they are consecutive trials from the same sessions), the five deltas share a common baseline, and no correction across the five tests is applied. The p-values should be read as descriptive.
- Ensemble 1's delta distribution is strongly asymmetric: its median is +0.07 but five trials are flagged as low outliers (down to −4.4 bins), so its mean is negative and t = −0.68. "No effect" is the right reading, but not "a symmetric null centred just above zero".
- The ablated population is 1140 units pooled across 13 task and 4 control-1 animals; 473 of them (41%) are control units, and ensembles 1, 2, 3 and 5 are roughly half control. This is not an ablation of a task-learned ensemble.
- The likelihood is Poisson in form but is evaluated on continuous firing rates rather than spike counts, with no exposure term (equivalent to assuming a 1 s exposure per bin); it is a scoring rule, not a calibrated likelihood, and it weights high-rate units more heavily than a count model would.
- Ensemble sizes differ (175–267 units), so knock-outs remove different numbers of units; no size-matched control knock-out (e.g. random units of equal count) was run, so part of any delta is simply lost population size.
- The star on ensemble 2 marks a significant *improvement* on removal. A caption that describes the panel as "ensembles required for decoding" would misreport it.
- The y-axis is labelled "Δ decoding error" with no unit; the unit is spatial bins (1 bin = 5 cm).
- This is a decoder-sufficiency analysis. **No physiological manipulation exists in this project**; the language must never reach "necessary".

### Fig3D_ensemble_single_decoders

**Shows.** The complement of the ablation: box plots of absolute decoding error per aligned trial for decoders built from single ensembles, against the full-population decoder ("full") and a shuffle control. All five single-ensemble decoders sit below the shuffle level — median errors 12.11, 11.72, 8.68, 5.29 and 6.62 bins for ensembles 1–5, against a shuffle median of ≈16.6 bins — but only ensembles 3, 4 and 5 do so cleanly; the per-trial distributions of ensembles 1 and 2 reach up into and past the shuffle box (ensemble 1's upper whisker is 18.8 bins). None approaches the full decoder's 3.83 bins (19 cm). The ordering mirrors the ablation exactly: ensembles 4 and 5, whose removal costs most, are also the best single decoders, and ensembles 1 and 2, whose removal costs nothing, are the worst. The panel therefore shows that position information is distributed across all five components but concentrated in two, and that no single ensemble is sufficient on its own.

**Methods.** Same decoder, tensor and cross-validation as `Fig3D_ensemble_ablation_delta`; the single-ensemble decoder for ensemble *e* retained only units with assignment = *e* (`decode_ensemble_ablation.m:126-128`), giving 236, 267, 175, 206 and 256 units. The shuffle control randomised the bin order independently for every unit and every trial (`:92-99`), destroying spatial tuning while preserving each unit's rate distribution, over 50 iterations; the box plotted is the per-trial mean across those 50 iterations, and the run log records shuffle RMSE 20.36 ± 0.05 bins (mean ± SEM over the 50 iterations) against a baseline RMSE of 8.30 (`processed_data/regen_chain2_2026-08-11.log`). Boxes summarise the 30 per-trial mean absolute errors, in the order full, shuffle, ensembles 1–5 (`ensemble_analysis.m:1096-1101`). Medians for the ensemble and full boxes were recomputed from an independent reimplementation on the cached tensor; the shuffle summary is not exported numerically, so its level was independently re-estimated with 10 fresh shuffle iterations (per-trial median 16.68 bins, RMSE 20.42, SD 0.35), consistent with both the plotted box and the logged RMSE. Figure emitted 2026-08-12 00:29 as `figures/ensemble_59_decoding_single_boxplot.png/.svg`.

**Caveats.**
- No statistical test is attached to this panel — no comparison against shuffle, against the full decoder, or between ensembles. The separations are descriptive only, and for ensembles 1 and 2 the separation from shuffle is small relative to trial-to-trial spread.
- The shuffle box is a mean over 50 iterations plotted alongside single-realisation boxes, so its spread (visibly the narrowest in the figure) is a standard-error-like quantity and is not comparable to the other boxes' trial-to-trial spread.
- Ensembles differ in size by up to 1.5× (267 vs 175 units), and decoding accuracy rises with unit count, so part of the ranking reflects population size rather than information content; no size-matched random-subset decoder was run.
- Same session-global leave-one-trial-out template, same n = 30 pooled-supermouse inference unit, same 41% control-unit composition, and same Poisson-form-on-rates scoring rule as the ablation panel.
- The occupancy-denominator defect (`spatial_binning.m:45`) inflates rates in a speed-dependent manner; to the extent that speed varies systematically along the corridor, part of the decodability of position is attributable to a speed-correlated rate artefact rather than to spatial tuning per se. (The corridor speed profile itself was not re-measured here.)
